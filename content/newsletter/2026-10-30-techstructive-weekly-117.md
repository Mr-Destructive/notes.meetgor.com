<h2>Week #117</h2>
<p>It was a busy week after a festive week. I worked on with an agent trying to replace code with an LLM call, that was weird but there are a lot of things I can learn from that. Like setting up so that an agent can make the transition better, having the understanding of the problem and actually able to solve it. That level of skill I haven’t yet gotten. Maybe I am too narrow-minded and still working with the mindset of walking when riding a race-car right? Working with agents is like being in a race car but not able to fully use it, because we haven’t fully appreciated the normal car enough?</p>
<p>That’s what I want to learn. I don’t think this week or the upcoming weekends are peaceful. Since its Diwali next weekend, and this weekend, it will be cleaning and chores, so reading is what I would prefer in pockets of spare time.</p>
<h3>Quote of the Week</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>“Darkness cannot drive out darkness, only light can do that. Hate cannot drive out hate, only love can do that”</p>
<p>— <a href="https://www.goodreads.com/quotes/943-darkness-cannot-drive-out-darkness-only-light-can-do-that">Martin Luther King Jr.</a></p>
</blockquote>
<p>True. Darkness is not something that can stand on its own, its not a thing, its a lack of light. Hatred is not a feeling, its a lack of love or compassion towards something or someone. If there is absence then it can only be filled with it and nothing else, or not more of the lackness.</p>
<p>The argument “Tit for tat” doesn’t work all the time, it can backfire if someone is in turnmoil or hasn’t gotten enough love, a adult in depression cannot be neglected, he might have not gotten love in the childhood. We cannot lighten up something by turning off more lights. If you have a choice to light up or blow out a hope, always choose lighting up.</p>

<h2>Read</h2>
<ol>
<li>
<p><a href="https://danluu.com/ambitious/">Danluu Blog: We should be more ambitious with software</a></p>
<ol>
<li><p>This is the general trend that we are all observing. Being more ambitious, being more productive, setting bigger goals and tasks, dreaming big. Being fast is also a new metric in software I think. This is not wrong, like AIs are making grunt work just go away with a simple words, or even speaking. You can just ask it and it will do it.</p></li>
<li><p>But, what about understanding. Does speed comes without knowing the tradeoffs? Maybe that is the standard expectations now. </p></li>
</ol>
</li>
<li>
<p><a href="https://gopher.muffinlabs.com/sdf.org/users/nervtoji/phlog/001.txt">Why Gopher in 2026</a></p>
<ol>
<li><p>Why even bother going deep right? Why even understand how a browser or DNS works, or how SSL and certification of HTTP works? Oh you don’t know that 4 can be a weak random number for certification encryption? Oh you are not a programmer, you are a brogrammer!</p></li>
<li><p>I think this is important, everyone is now able to expand their breadth of knowledge, as a developer the disctinction would be to have a depth of knowledge about something, it might seem its redundant to know it, considering how quickly these models are able to do what they are told, but I think it gives a edge over the assumptions the model might make.</p></li>
</ol>
</li>
<li>
<p><a href="https://felixrieseberg.com/hunger-beats-tenure-advice-for-junior-engineers/">Hunger beats Tenure</a></p>
<ol>
<li><p>Good points here. Curiosity to show empathy from users perspective or eagerness to solve something. I think this has gone on steroids now. But interviewing is a different ball game right? How can that be tested in small duration of time? Designing something impossible is just like asking to test his/her problem solving skills and ability to think through a solution. Is that not different from what is the driving force for the candidate to work at that company?</p></li>
<li><p>I think the test should be of what the candidate has built already and why and challenge his/her stance and make him contradict his own idea or approach, can you be squared in your own ambitions and built craft?</p></li>
</ol>
</li>
</ol>
